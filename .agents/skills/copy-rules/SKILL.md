# Copy rules

All text on the site must read like a person explaining a product to a colleague. Short, plain, direct.

## When to use this

- Building the site from scratch: write all text following these rules.
- Adding a section or changing a sentence: follow these rules for the new text only. Do not rewrite text the user did not ask about.
- Fixed copy (see below) stays exactly as written unless the user asks to change it.

## Do

- Say what the product is and does in the first sentence of each section.
- Use concrete nouns: "machine", "GPU", "model", "data", "electricity".
- Keep sentences under 20 words. Use periods and commas. Use a colon when listing.
- Use active voice. "You buy the machine once." not "The machine is purchased once."
- Write "you" and "your" for the customer.
- Keep headings short, 2 to 5 words, in sentence case.
- Name the real trade-off when there is one. Example: open models are a little behind the frontier, but close enough for most work.

## Do not

- No em dashes and no en dashes. Use a comma, period or colon.
- No hype words: revolutionize, unleash, seamless, cutting-edge, next-generation, empower, elevate, supercharge, game-changing, leverage, robust, transform, unlock, journey, world-class, state-of-the-art.
- Do not start a sentence with "Imagine". Do not end a section with a question.
- No exclamation marks.
- No emojis.
- No triplets of vague adjectives ("fast, secure, powerful").
- No claims with numbers that are not already on the site or in AGENTS.md. No prices, speeds, benchmarks, customer names, quotes, awards.
- Do not say "AI-powered", "AI-driven" or "in today's fast-paced world".

The words "agent harness" are allowed. They are part of the product description.

## Before and after

Bad: "Unleash the power of cutting-edge AI with our seamless, robust solutions."
Good: "We sell a machine that runs an AI model in your office. You buy it once."

Bad: "Imagine a world where your data never leaves your building."
Good: "Your data stays in your building. Nothing is sent to an outside API."

Bad: "Our game-changing pricing model empowers businesses."
Good: "You pay once for the hardware. After that you pay for electricity."

## Fixed copy

- Wordmark above the tagline: "WarpDancer"
- Tagline: "Stop renting your intelligence. Own it." The word "renting" is red (`.renting`) and the word "Own" is green (`.highlight`).
- Hero paragraph: "The big AI labs are like Uber. If you drive every day, you buy a car. We sell ready-made local AI machines, so you buy the hardware once and then pay only for electricity."
- Button: "Book a call"
- Footer: "WarpDancer" and the current year.

## Ideas from the pitch (use when writing new sections)

- Big AI labs are like Uber: cheap and easy at the start, then the price goes up. Netflix did the same.
- If you drive every day, you buy a car. It is always there, your data stays with you, and you can change how it works.
- Local AI works the same way. You set it up once and own it. You pay for electricity and upkeep, not for the intelligence.
- Be honest about the limits: AI prices might not rise much, and open models are a little behind the frontier.

## Check

After writing text, search the file for these and fix every hit:

- the characters for an em dash and an en dash
- every word from the banned list above
- "LIVE", "NEW", "BETA"
- any number that was not already on the site or in AGENTS.md
